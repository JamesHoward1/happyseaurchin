SECTION 2: OPPORTUNITY STATEMENT

The gap is coordination across organisations nobody owns; the beach is early to it, unmeasured, with no entity.

The problem or opportunity, and what happens if we do nothing.

2.11UK organisations are adopting AI widely and shallowly: 29% of businesses use it, at an average of 1.6 tools each, and six in ten name skills as the barrier (Matthew's commissioned research, 10 October). The recommended fix, a governed assistant connected step by step to existing systems and measured, is sound inside one organisation. It leaves three things missing: each person's assistant starts from nothing, connecting systems does not connect people, and the capability already in people stays unseen.

2.12Those gaps bite hardest where work crosses organisational lines. Multi-agency partnerships, member networks and startup cohorts run on email, a WhatsApp group and a shared drive one party owns, because the first question is always whose system it goes in. The published research on multi-agency work says information sharing is slow or refused by default, roles are unclear and nobody has time.

2.13The opportunity is narrow: a shared surface that nobody owns, where each person keeps their own account and any AI assistant reads across all of them through one connector. That gap is opening now as persistent personal assistants (OpenAI's Dots from 29 September, Claude, Copilot) become ordinary and each lab keeps memory inside its own walls. David Pinto's beach is early to it, open source under MIT, and used daily by a small community.

2.14If we do nothing: the beach stays a set of claims with no number behind it, with ten candidate products and more fronts than people. David's own fallback is teaching. The one external deployment has already paused because its user could not run it without him. The gap will be filled by someone else, or by nobody, and six weeks of unpaid work on my side produces no result either way.

Current state.

2.21Technology live: a federated server of pscale blocks, an MCP connector, identity keys, opt-in encryption. It has survived a full platform migration.

2.22Product: not chosen. Ten candidates; the buyer-first research (9 October) found only two reach an invoice this year, the facilitated meeting method with the fold as its notebook, and a £5 RPG seat.

2.23Measurement: none. Nine tests designed by David's side, five by me, none run. No controlled comparison anywhere.

2.24Revenue: low hundreds, from Community Recovery. Robert's backing was accepted on 4 October, with two records that do not agree: I was told £1,000, released on my validation; David's side records the sum as unwritten and the form as EIS. EIS needs a qualifying company issuing shares, and there is no company, so as recorded it cannot be made. The sum, the form and what my validation means all need writing down; until an entity exists, the form has to be something other than EIS.

2.25Entity: none. Ecosquared Ltd in strike-off; David left Sqale Ltd in August. Licence answered (MIT); code ownership, Sqale's rights and the invoicing vehicle unanswered.

2.26Data posture: public by default, encryption opt-in. No DPIA, no Online Safety Act assessment.

2.27Team: David, his agents, Matthew as convener, me on step 0 unpaid.

2.28Two designs for the test: David's Silo Buster steps (keel's summary, 11 October) and Matthew's Capability Gap pilot (10 October). Both agreed on 11 October that they should become one test. The experiment document does that.

2.29My position: Tape Up verdict delivered (decline, at most one Sparring Session). David's side keeps casting me as pilot contact, verifier and commercial route.

Target state: by 7 November, one of four verdicts on paper, from one group that is not David's collaborators.

2.31Go: the group ran its second week without a facilitator's hands, the three message counts beat their baseline, a fresh assistant answers four of five plain questions from the shared surface alone, and the convener names a price from their own budget. A second site is scheduled and the next cycle priced.

2.32Reshape: the method works and the beach adds nothing a shared page would not; sell the method, drop the substrate. Or the beach works and nobody will pay; reframe as a commons and research asset with a foundation behind it.

2.33Stop: the group could not run without David or the facilitator. Inventor to keep warm.

2.34Founder-negative: no private default by day one, or the pilot displaced by a new front. Stop.

2.35In every case: predictions written before day one, result published including a loss, and the ownership questions answered in writing. Outputs: the result on the beach beside the Capability Gap page, a five-minute demo if Go, and a facilitator-runnable version of the fold (template, prompt, private beach) as the first repeatable product if Go.

Which objectives or strategic goals this aligns with.

2.41For me: the SVOS thesis, tested on a partnership instead of a company. Coordination out of people's heads and into a system the group owns, with the founder (here, David) removed from the loop as the test of whether it holds. A cross-organisation coordination workshop is a sellable MLC service whether or not the beach wins. The Tape Up stands: this is step 0 of the side bet, unpaid, and nothing beyond it without pay.

2.42For David: his own axiom B. A structure holds only when the group carries it without its author. Unassisted return is the one measurement his system cannot refuse. His six Silo Buster steps each have a place in the pilot, including his hour with the people the day-two reading finds.

2.43For Matthew: the Capability Gap's stated pilot, merged with David's steps as he agreed, with one addition (facilitator steps back in week two), and a result he can show a chief executive.

2.44Does not align with, and does not propose: a skunk works across five fronts, a raise, the RPG, the mirror, the bobble, or vector money.

2.45On the £230,000 figure: Matthew's Capability Gap page estimates £230,000 a year for an organisation of fifty, from published figures and stated assumptions, and labels it as an estimate. This proposal does not count it as a benefit. The pilot can put a measured number under one line of it, time spent finding things out, and the page could show that number beside the estimate once it exists. How the page presents it is Matthew's call.

SECTION 3: IMPLEMENTATION

Four moves, this proposal funds the third: one number from one group.

Approach: the theory of how this wins, in four moves, each conditional on the last.

3.11The wedge is one app for one weekly job, not the substrate. Bluesky carried AT Protocol; the protocol did not carry itself. The fold for a bounded multi-organisation group with a deadline is the wedge, because it is the one job where everyone writes, nobody edits anyone else, one read, is the feature.

3.12Facilitators are the carrier. Ninety per cent of any community lurks; nobody writes without a ritual and a visible consequence. The first groups are run by a person who reads the fold aloud. Facilitators are to the beach what game masters are to a table.

3.13One number before anything else. One group, a control or its own baseline, predictions written first, result published including a loss. Then a five-minute demo nobody else can do (a stranger's assistant leaves a note in your room, across two vendors, zero setup, with the time beside it). Then the people who have tried to make a commons stick.

3.14The standard play comes last. Once two vendors' assistants have met on it in real work, the addressing scheme becomes the convention and the beach the reference implementation, held by a foundation or CIC. That fits David's principles better than a limited company and answers the entity question without asking him to run one.

3.15This proposal funds move 3 only. The pilot brings together David's Silo Buster steps and Matthew's ten-day Capability Gap design, as both agreed on 11 October, with one addition: the facilitator steps back on day six. In week one, the day-two reading looks for outliers, David holds the room once with the people it finds, and the group runs one Action Cycle on a real pain point. In week two, David and the facilitator are absent. Credits and money do not move inside the pilot. The full design, including where each of David's steps sits, is at the pilot design.

Adjacencies.

3.21Upstream: Matthew's Capability Gap page and research (10 October); David's Silo Buster steps (keel's summary at pool:emojisword, 11 October); the Tape Up (8 October); the buyer-first research (9 October); my step 0 answer and the meld with David on the beach (13 October).

3.22Downstream if Go: site 2; a priced second cycle; the facilitator-runnable private beach; the demo; the outreach list.

3.23Beside it, not in it: David's RPG, mirror and agents; Robert's backing; the V.C Tape Up process, where I remain reviewer.

3.24In the SVOS canon: sits at System and Data Foundations for the group (one shared record), but reads Governance and Decision Architecture first (who decides, who is accountable, written down). The pilot tests whether a system can run ahead of the people and process it instruments, which SVOS says it cannot.

Dependencies: before day one, all four sites.

3.31Private by default for any group writing anything about a person. Encryption exists in the code; the default is wrong for every paying buyer.

3.32A data protection impact assessment before any personal data, and an Online Safety Act illegal-content assessment before anything social. Days of work, not weeks, and both overdue.

3.33The ownership question in writing: who owns the code, what Sqale Ltd holds, which vehicle invoices, and which vehicle takes Robert's money.

3.34A person who is not David has built a working page over the group's block from the public conventions alone (keel's T6), in three working days or fewer. If not, the pilot waits and T6 is the finding.

3.35Step 0 done: David, Matthew, Julie and Mark have answered the three questions, as I did on 13 October.

Sites, by option. Options, not a fixed sequence; the adoption path at 3.47 says how they connect.

3.41Option A, site 1: a client leadership team through a leadership-development partner who already runs leadership programmes, CEO preparation and design sprints, and could run the room with David.

3.42Option A, site 2: a leadership or management team in a company whose people belong to a Sheffield AI community, with a Copilot user on one side as the cross-vendor check.

3.43Option A, the caution: run with a leadership team of peers, it is coordination among equals, not employee evaluation. Widening it to the teams they lead needs the data protection impact assessment and the data rule first, because readiness-by-department read across a workforce is evaluation under a power imbalance.

3.44Option B, site 3: Community Recovery, Northamptonshire (Matthew). Multi-organisation by nature, the only organisation that has paid David, pilot already designed. Staff from three to five organisations; no service users write; no service-user identifier in any block. A members' room on the mirror is a different test and waits for privacy by default.

3.45Option B, site 4: a member network that is not Venture.Community. Highest-scoring segment in the research, no conflict for me: a Sheffield AI community, a Sheffield Chamber patron circle, an IoD special interest group.

3.46Option B, site 5: startups working together, as a V.C power-of-the-network play. John Holmes reviewing as a Fellow. The decision sits with John or Pete, not me. The first risk, said in the room on 9 October, is that the startups have no time; that is the kill criterion.

3.47The adoption path: buyers and deciders first. A leadership team tries it on itself, gives feedback and engages before anyone else is asked. If count 4 holds, it widens to the teams they lead, then to the partners they work with, which is where Option B begins. The research's caution about single organisations still stands for the notebook (a vault with an assistant on top beats the beach inside one owner's walls); what a leadership team takes on is the method and the immediate read, and the beach earns its place as the work widens beyond one system.

3.5Timelines and effort: ten working days per site plus a week either side. XS per site in facilitator days; S (1 to 3 months) for the four-site sequence if site 1 returns Go.

Key stakeholders.

3.61David Pinto: substrate holder. Fixes the platform if it breaks; holds the room once in week one with the people the reading finds. Does not write for the group, train, or attend in week two. His agents (keel, weft) do not write for the group either.

3.62Matthew Bushell: convener of site 1, keeper of the Capability Gap page.

3.63The site's convener and participants, and the host's information governance lead.

3.64John Holmes and Pete Hopton: decide and verify any V.C site.

3.65A leadership-development partner: the Option A route, and a possible facilitator alongside David.

3.66Robert: backer, accepted 4 October. Sum and form to be written down (see current state). Owed a plain statement of what was and was not validated.

3.7Resources required: budget £500 per site, for hosting and inference. One facilitator (me, where no conflict; otherwise a named person who is not David), about two days a week for two weeks per site plus day-one setup. One scorer (me; John or Pete for V.C). One engineer, three days, for T6, who has never spoken to David and is not me. No IT or vendor support beyond David's platform fixes. The host organisation's information governance lead, one hour.

Known risks and constraints.

3.81Single maintainer: delivery stops when David steps away. Mitigated only by T6 passing.

3.82Data: public by default on a substrate whose first site is a recovery partnership. Mitigated by private-by-default, the data rule (first names and organisations only), and the DPIA; kill switch on any identifiable third-party entry.

3.83No entity: nothing to contract with. First revenue is somebody else's revenue, and Robert's backing, recorded as EIS, cannot be made in that form without a company. Not mitigated; surfaced as a precondition.

3.84Participation: expect 1 to 10 per cent writing by week two without a designed ritual. Mitigated by the Monday prompt and the fold read aloud; measured by count 4.

3.85Hidden hands and the room as evidence: keel or weft writing for the group voids the result; David's week-one hour may go well, and a good room is not count 4.

3.86Front sprawl: the RPG, the mirror or a new tool eating the fortnight. Facilitator and David hours logged by day.

3.87My conflict: I am V.C's reviewer on David's venture. I design and score; I do not sign, invoice or verify any V.C site. Invoicing never runs through Sparring Ventures for anything touching V.C.

3.88Polite interest read as validation: only the convener's day-ten answer on money counts.

3.89Vocabulary tax: the admin tax is replaced by a vocabulary tax (spine, pool, passport, now, hand, fold, mirror, shell). Today nobody uses it without David translating. T6 is the test; the facilitator absorbs it in the pilot.

SECTION 4: WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

Four counts, predictions written and dated before day one.

4.1Count 1: messages spent asking where someone is with a piece of work, against the previous fortnight. Pass lower; kill no change or higher.

4.2Count 2: messages and hours spent finding a time to meet, against the last meeting arranged the old way. Pass lower; kill no change or higher.

4.3Count 3: plain questions a fresh assistant answers correctly from the shared surface alone, out of five. Pass four or more on day ten; kill two or fewer.

4.4Count 4: share of participants writing their own weekly lines in days six to ten with no chase beyond the Monday prompt. Pass 40% or more; kill under 20%.

4.5Count 4 is the one that matters: the other three can be true with a facilitator in the room. It is also David's first measure, who comes back without being chased. Also recorded: facilitator and David hands by day; convention mistakes; AI usage cost; open-meeting attendance; action cycles run; peer acknowledgements; the convener's day-ten answer on money.

4.6Success for the proposal, as distinct from the pilot, is a verdict on paper, whichever of the four it is.

SECTION 5: COST AND BENEFIT

£500 to £2,000 cash and five unpaid operator days for site 1; a verdict either way.

5.1Cost to serve, site 1: facilitator days (setup, days 1 to 5, scoring, write-up), 5 days unpaid at step 0, £0 cash and 5 days opportunity cost. Engineer for the T6 gate, 3 days, not me and not David, £0 to £1,500 depending on who. Hosting and inference, 10 days for 10 people, capped at £500. Convener and participants, 20 minutes each on day one and 10 minutes a week after, their own time. David's hour with the outliers, his own time. DPIA and OSA assessment, 2 days on David's side, overdue regardless. Total for site 1: £500 to £2,000 cash, and 5 unpaid operator days.

5.2Comparator: the one Sparring Session the Tape Up allows would cost the same operator time and produce a scope, not a number.

5.3If Go: a number that is not David's; a priced second cycle (the research's anchor is £750 for a partnership brief, then £50 a month per partnership); a demo; a reason for the outreach list to look at a zero-star repo; and a facilitator-runnable product with a repeatable buyer at £1,000 to £2,000 a year per facilitator. For MLC: a cross-organisation coordination workshop that sells on its own paper.

5.4If Reshape or Stop: the side bet closed on evidence at a cost of five days, instead of drifting into unpaid go-to-market. The method is sellable either way. If Founder-negative: the same, faster.

5.5The Capability Gap's estimate (£230,000 a year for fifty people) is not counted here. It is an estimate from published figures and stated assumptions, and this pilot is the first thing that could put a measured number under one line of it: time spent finding things out.

5.6Decision asked for: approve step 0 at the fence already set. Unpaid to day ten of site 1, published result, then a go/no-go on paid work. Nothing beyond site 1 without a day rate or the beach paying.
